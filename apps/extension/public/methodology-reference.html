Overview and system boundary

EcoLogits is the current calculation method for all supported sites. The browser uses the project's independent TypeScript port of the accepted pinned EcoLogits core and catalog; the organization server uses the corresponding native Python implementation. Output tokens, usable observed timing and reviewed model mappings feed operational estimates. Missing inputs and unsupported work stay explicit. Consumer-to-API mappings and provider environments are assumptions, not verified deployment facts.
The project-developed Original calculation was previously used and was archived on 22 September 2026. Its literature review, source audits, numerical checks, independent reviews and corrections are preserved in the Original method research archive. Its code remains available for reproducibility and saved records. The worked example, fixed four-profile cohort and separate water-component rules below document that historical reference method. They do not define the current EcoLogits catalog selection.
EcoLogits uses a reviewed direct model mapping where available, otherwise an explicit provider flagship or benchmark proxy. The product does not automatically fall back to the archived method. Electricity, operational carbon and upstream water-consumption ranges retain their endpoints. The Range display setting shows the midpoint scenario by default, with minimum and maximum available; these are scenarios, not established statistical confidence limits. Full upstream results and attribution remain attached to the method, while the headline footprint excludes embodied manufacturing impacts.

AI Footprint Tracker estimates the operational footprint of supported AI activity. It starts with observable work, such as a submitted message, an answer, an image-generation event or accessible document content. A workload-specific model estimates electricity. Separate factors translate the appropriate electricity quantities into electricity-related greenhouse-gas emissions and consumptive water use. These are estimated quantities associated with a declared scope, not direct measurements of a particular provider’s infrastructure. [1] [6] [7]

The calculation distinguishes three electricity boundaries: accelerator electricity, serving-IT electricity, and facility electricity. Accelerators are the processors that perform most model computations. Serving IT additionally includes an allocated share of the host computer. Facility electricity adds cooling, power conversion and other data-center overhead represented by a compatible power-usage-effectiveness factor. A GPU-only benchmark cannot be called facility electricity merely by changing its label. [1] [6] [9]

Training, manufacturing the AI hardware, the employee’s computer and the employee’s network are excluded from the headline footprint. This is the product’s operational boundary, not a claim that those activities have no impact. A source that combines operation and manufacturing cannot be inserted into this calculation without separating those contributions. Electricity-supply factors may themselves include upstream generation processes; their definition is retained rather than silently relabeled as direct power-station emissions. [6] [7] [5]

The base units are kilowatt-hours for electricity, grams of carbon-dioxide equivalent for emissions, and litres for water consumption. Display conversions do not change the estimate: kWh × 1,000 gives Wh; L × 1,000 gives mL; kg CO₂e × 1,000 gives g CO₂e. Joules convert to kWh by division by 3,600,000, because a watt is a joule per second and an hour contains 3,600 seconds. These are unit conversions, not empirical model coefficients. [1] [9]

Tokens and conversation context

A tokenizer divides text into the units used by a language model. A token is not necessarily a word: punctuation, parts of words, spaces and non-Latin characters can be represented differently by different encodings. For a supplied text string s and a particular tokenizer T, its count is the length of T(s). A pinned tokenizer can give a reproducible count for that string without proving that the string includes everything processed by the service. [16] [17]

N_text = length(T(s))

Counting prefers a provider-reported number only when its meaning and scope are verified. Otherwise it uses a verified tokenizer mapping, a calibrated local approximation, or an explicitly labeled rough profile. The tokenization profile records its model mapping, asset revision and normalization rules. Different tokenizer families are not made equivalent by applying one fixed correction percentage. Text is assembled before final counting because independently tokenizing stream fragments can give a different result from tokenizing their concatenation. [16] [17] [22]

The browser audit of 9 September 2026 found observable prompts and answer representations on the four websites supported then, but no verified complete numerical input/output usage report in the inspected samples. Claude quota percentages, ChatGPT stop-token identifiers, Perplexity first-token booleans and undocumented Gemini positional values are not substitutes for a verified token-usage field. Exposed reasoning summaries also do not reveal the number of hidden reasoning tokens. Later checks found usage reports for Claude Cowork results and Claude AI artifact runs; the section on reply parts, generated media and shared chats describes how they are used. [22]

A new message in an old conversation

For a new request, earlier prompts and answers may be reused as input context. The conversation’s age does not exclude that text. The new input estimate therefore considers the retained active branch and the new prompt, while new output means the newly generated answer. Reopening the same saved conversation does not create a new generation. Previously untracked historical requests are not silently added to the current month. [22]

Estimated current input = tokenized retained context + new prompt + known non-overlapping input components

This expression describes component accounting, not permission to sum independently tokenized fragments under an arbitrary serialization. The concrete tokenizer profile defines message framing, special tokens and component overlap. The full serialized input should be counted when it is actually available. Provider-side summarization, retrieval, hidden instructions and caching can make the visible transcript differ from the retained model input. [17] [20] [22]

Context-window guidance uses the current estimated active context, not cumulative tokens spent across every earlier request. Its comparison budget comes from a verified consumer-app limit or a disclosed advisory budget. The above-50% notification is AI Footprint Tracker’s guidance policy, not a scientific threshold for environmental harm or proof that the model is out of space. It never automatically changes the prompt or starts another conversation.

Why the extension does not call a remote counter for live chats

Anthropic provides a free, authenticated, rate-limited input-counting endpoint. It can be used with licensed public or synthetic material to compare local tokenizers during development. It does not return historical Claude.ai consumption, simulate cache hits, or establish the hidden output usage of a past response. Employee prompts, answers and documents are not sent to this endpoint for routine counting. [17]

Electricity equations

The transparent starting reference is the operational component of EcoLogits 0.11.1, published on 7 July 2026 and pinned to commit 886d1ebfadb51a2d6407fcea6d9778c997c85c35. The tracker now uses the corrected model catalog from EcoLogits 0.11.2, commit c739e8d61f6a12a85864adb698ab85623fe70f3c, verified on 30 September 2026. The calculation equations are unchanged; the worked reference below remains reproducible with its original inputs. The code separates GPU energy, model-memory allocation, non-GPU host energy and facility overhead. These equations do not establish that a closed provider uses the assumed hardware or deployment settings. [1] [2]

The energy expression returns one modeled GPU’s allocation for the request. Batch size is already present in the fitted GPU term, so dividing this term by B a second time would be an error. EcoLogits’ fitting-data selection uses H100 80 GB measurements, batch sizes no greater than 512 and model sizes no greater than 200 billion parameters. Memory and allocated GPUs

M_required [GB] = 1.2 × P_t × q / 8 g = 2 ^ ceil(log₂(ceil(M_required / M_gpu)))

The multiplier 1.2 is the reference’s memory-overhead heuristic. It is not a multiplier for cooling or electricity. The GPU count rounds the required allocation up to a power of two. This is a reference topology, not evidence that the provider used that many GPUs. The default weight precision is 16 bits and the reference memory is 80 GB per GPU. [1]

Duration and non-GPU host electricity

t_reg [seconds] = n × (a_L × P_a + b_L × B + c_L) t = min(t_request, t_reg + TTFT) E_host [kWh] = (t / 3,600) × P_host × (g / G_server) / B

If a validated throughput value is selected, n / TPS replaces the regression duration. TTFT denotes time to first token. The pinned kernel uses zero as the absent TTFT default and an unbounded request-duration cap when no request latency is supplied. The method identity must record whether it uses regression duration, throughput data, first-token timing and a request-duration cap. Switching among them changes the answer. [1] [3]

The default non-GPU host power is 1.2 kW, the reference server has 8 GPUs, and the batch size is 64. This host allocation represents aggregate non-GPU serving energy. It is not a separately measured CPU or RAM contribution. Browser elapsed time is not measured processor time: it can contain queueing, network delays and tool work. In this reference, elapsed time can cap a prediction; it must not be relabeled as an instrument reading. [1] [6]

IT and facility electricity

E_IT = E_host + g × E_GPU,one PUE = facility electricity / IT electricity E_facility = E_IT × PUE

Power usage effectiveness, or PUE, represents facility overhead relative to IT electricity. A source that already reports facility electricity is used directly at that boundary; multiplying it by PUE again would double-count overhead. IT energy may be recovered from facility energy only when a compatible PUE or a separately reported overhead component is available. [1] [6]

What the reference does not resolve

This is an output-token-based text-inference surrogate, not an isolated physical measurement of decoding. Its benchmark-normalized energy can include average prompt-processing work, but its GPU equation has no explicit input-token variable. With the fixed reference settings, changing input length alone does not change the energy estimate. Counting retained conversation context improves token accounting and guidance, but does not make this energy model input-aware. Hidden reasoning, reserved idle capacity and complete auxiliary-service work also remain unverified. [1] [6]

A more detailed candidate model separates prompt processing, often called prefill, from output generation. The measurement structure is E_total = E_prefill + E_decode, with device components such as GPU, CPU and DRAM measured over the appropriate phases. This decomposition is supported by measurement work, but it supplies no universal proprietary-model coefficients. An input-aware replacement needs benchmark data, held-out validation and a declared transfer limitation before it replaces the transparent baseline. [11] [8]

Electricity-related carbon emissions

C_electricity [g CO₂e] = E_facility [kWh] × I_electricity [g CO₂e/kWh]

The electricity factor expresses greenhouse-gas impact per unit of electricity. CO₂e combines relevant greenhouse gases using the source’s equivalence method. The factor’s geography, time period and life-cycle boundary must be recorded. A location-based factor describes an electricity mix; a market-based factor reflects qualifying electricity procurement and contractual attributes. They are different accounting views and are not silently mixed. [6] [10] [5]

Google’s study reports 2024 fleet factors of 345 g CO₂e/kWh for its location-based view and 94 g CO₂e/kWh for its market-based view. Applied to the study’s rounded 0.24 Wh reference, these give 0.0828 g and 0.02256 g of electricity-related emissions respectively. Neither multiplication reproduces the study’s published 0.03 g total, which also includes additional Scope 1 and Scope 3 contributions. These are dated comparisons of boundaries, not default factors for every AI tool. [6]

AI Footprint Tracker does not use an employee’s IP address as the location of the AI-serving data center. Where the deployment is not disclosed, it uses a named environmental scenario with an explicit source and date. The result is described as scenario-based electricity-related CO₂e, not verified emissions from the actual server. Renewable-electricity procurement does not make physical electricity use or water consumption disappear. [6] [7]

Direct cooling and electricity-supply water

Water withdrawal is water taken from a source. Water consumption is the portion not returned within the source’s defined accounting boundary, for example because of evaporation. The tracker reports consumption, not withdrawal. A litre of consumption also does not express local water scarcity or ecological harm; those would require additional location-specific characterization. [7] [6]

W_direct [L] = E_IT [kWh] × WUE_IT [L/kWh_IT] W_electricity [L] = E_facility [kWh] × EWIF [L/kWh] W_total [L] = W_direct + W_electricity

WUE_IT is direct consumptive water per unit of IT electricity. EWIF is electricity-supply water consumption per unit of supplied electricity. They use different electricity denominators. Li and colleagues express the same operational relationship as a sum over time: IT energy multiplied by direct WUE plus PUE multiplied by electricity-supply intensity. The EcoLogits kernel implements this denominator-aware relationship. [7] [1]

W_operation = Σ_t E_IT,t × (WUE_IT,t + PUE_t × EWIF_t) EWIF_t = Σ_k (generation_k,t × water_intensity_k) / Σ_k generation_k,t

The second expression weights electricity-generation technologies by their contribution to the electricity mix. A factor for direct cooling cannot stand in for electricity-generation water, and a withdrawal factor cannot stand in for a consumption factor. Where a source defines direct WUE using facility electricity instead of IT electricity, that definition requires its own formula and metadata rather than reuse of the IT equation. [7]

Google’s production study uses consumptive freshwater WUE of 1.15 L/kWh of IT electricity for both 2023 and 2024. Using the rounded published IT component, (0.24 − 0.02) Wh × 1.15 L/kWh gives approximately 0.253 mL, close to its published 0.26 mL result based on unrounded data. That figure concerns direct cooling. It does not provide a total including electricity-generation water. [6]

EcoLogits mode reports its Water Consumption Footprint (WCF) range as estimated water consumption. Like electricity and carbon, the figure shown is one end of that range, chosen by the Range display setting: the midpoint, (minimum + maximum) / 2, by default, or the minimum or maximum scenario when selected. Totals add the same end of every record's range. It does not separately recalculate cooling and electricity-supply water. The following component rules apply to the Original calculation: total water is reported only when both required components are available under compatible definitions. If one is unknown, the available component remains visible but is not mislabeled as total water. The same environmental scenario is carried through the calculation; a low-energy cooling configuration and an unrelated high-water cooling configuration cannot be combined as though they describe one deployment. [7] [4]

Fallback for an unfamiliar model

When a model does not have an applicable reviewed profile, AI Footprint Tracker’s fallback policy chooses the highest electricity estimate among its eligible comparable profiles. This is a declared conservative selection rule within that set. It is not evidence that the selected proxy is the actual model, nor a guaranteed upper limit on the real service’s consumption.

M(x) = eligible comparable profiles for observed workload x m*(x) = argmax over m in M(x) of E_facility,m(x) Reported vector = [E, C, W_direct, W_electricity] from m*(x)

Comparability requires the same task class, compatible workload assumptions, compatible energy boundary and a documented environmental scenario. Each candidate is evaluated for the same observed workload. Selection uses facility electricity only when all candidate boundaries support it. Carbon and both water components then come from that same selected candidate and scenario. Independently choosing the highest carbon from one deployment and the highest water from another would create a combination that no candidate actually represents. [6] [7] [4]

The model cards verify the architecture families; the additional precision in the Meta parameter values comes from the pinned EcoLogits registry, not from a vendor statement giving those decimals. These are comparator profiles, not replacements for the names of the models used by employees. [14] [13] [12] [3]

Fallback numbers are labeled “Highest comparable estimate.” Details shows the selected proxy, cohort version, calculation scope, environmental assumptions and missing work. Hidden reasoning alone does not suppress an otherwise useful text estimate. However, a text-only profile is not used to fabricate an image-generation estimate, and an unknown amount of work is not changed to zero. If no comparable profile exists, a separately labeled benchmark reference may be shown; it is not added to usage totals as though it were a request-specific estimate.

Model registries also need source validation. The pinned EcoLogits entry for mistral-large-2512 describes a dense 123-billion-parameter model and cites older material, whereas Mistral identifies that version as a mixture-of-experts model with 675 billion total and 41 billion active parameters. That conflicting entry is excluded until corrected in an explicit data revision. Proprietary-model parameter ranges in a library are hypotheses unless supported by original architecture evidence; they are not empirical confidence intervals. [3] [15] [24]

Images, documents and other files

Image generation, image input and document input are different workloads. The first release includes tracking for supported image-generation flows, but a model’s presence in a menu does not establish a calibrated environmental profile. Each media estimate identifies whether it describes a matched workload, a generic task reference or only an available text component. Media energy is not obtained by applying a text-token coefficient to a file’s byte size. [10] [8]

Generating or editing an image

For a compatible benchmark with per-image energy e_image, the corresponding component is the number of generated images multiplied by e_image. Relevant conditions include the generation architecture, resolution, quality settings, image-editing versus new-generation mode, number of sampling/denoising steps where applicable, hardware and batching. Opening an existing image or downloading another thumbnail is not another image generation. [8] [10]

E_component [kWh] = N_generated_images × e_image [J/image] / 3,600,000

ML.ENERGY records can supply GPU-energy measurements, including steady-state joules. Such a record gives a GPU component, not automatically host or facility electricity. A CPU/RAM estimate, an idle-capacity allocation and a facility factor require compatible evidence. They must not be invented just to complete the table. A text-generated SVG or HTML graphic is also not automatically a raster-image-model generation. [9] [8]

Luccioni and colleagues’ study reports mean image-generation energy of 2.907 kWh per 1,000 inferences, numerically equivalent to 2.907 Wh per inference, with standard deviation 3.310 in the same normalized units. Its reported median is 1.35 Wh per inference, and a high case is 11.49 Wh per inference. These are different statistics from a particular study cohort, not interchangeable defaults or confidence bounds. The paper describes sequential inference on one A100-SXM4-80GB in AWS us-west-2, with CodeCarbon accounting for hardware components. [10]

The authors’ released records introduce an important qualification: an SDXL query-phase record reports 8 detected A100 GPUs, machine-level tracking and approximately 1,148.738 GB of RAM. It records 11.4787172 kWh and 1.5926700 kg CO₂e for 1,000 queries, implying approximately 138.75 g CO₂e/kWh, rather than the paper’s stated 297.6 g/kWh. The scripts do not explicitly pin resolution, denoising steps or model revision. Those boundary and configuration differences are why this study’s high case is not adopted as a universal image default. [25] [10]

Those historical figures demonstrate why image generation needs its own profiles. They are not calibrated coefficients for ChatGPT Images, Nano Banana or every Perplexity image model. The highest-comparable rule applies to an approved image cohort only after the task, units and boundaries are established. A cohort maximum without recoverable configuration metadata remains a benchmark reference rather than an automatic production default. [10] [8]

Comparison with the ML.ENERGY Leaderboard

The ML.ENERGY Leaderboard by Chung and colleagues publishes measured GPU energy per generated image for open text-to-image models such as FLUX.1-dev and HunyuanDiT, with the resolution, denoising steps, precision, GPU type, GPU count and batch size of each configuration. It is a useful external comparison for the per-image figure the tracker uses. The tracker does not bundle, copy or reproduce these measurements, and this page quotes none of them: the leaderboard repository has no licence file. See the leaderboard for its current values. [26] [27] [28]

Its figures are GPU energy within the benchmark’s measurement window, for specific open models and settings. They leave out the host computer, idle capacity and data-centre overhead, and they do not measure the image models behind ChatGPT, Gemini or Perplexity. The benchmark already normalizes GPU energy per generated image, so a comparison must not multiply it by the GPU count or divide it by the batch size again. A configuration with other settings does not receive an invented pixel or step multiplier. [26] [9]

If a separate environmental scenario is explicitly applied to the covered GPU component, the calculation is an allocation, not a measurement of complete image generation. The GPU energy is treated as the covered subset of IT energy; proportional facility overhead, electricity-related carbon and water are allocated to that subset. Host energy, production idle reserve and other unobserved work remain excluded. If the target’s settings do not support the comparison, the result is a reference scenario only, not a request-specific footprint. [6] [7] [9]

E_facility,covered = E_GPU,covered × PUE C_covered = E_facility,covered × I_electricity W_direct,covered = E_GPU,covered × WUE_IT W_electricity,covered = E_facility,covered × EWIF

The tracker applies no such allocation to leaderboard figures and adds none of them to any total. Generated images are estimated with the published average described in the section on reply parts and generated media; the leaderboard remains a comparison outside the figures. Until 20 October 2026 the tracker bundled a selection of these measurements as a reference shown apart from the totals; it was removed because the repository has no licence. [26] [28]

Uploading an image for analysis

Image input involves preprocessing and a visual representation in addition to any text answer. Official API documentation supplies some token-accounting rules, but these are not physical energy coefficients and do not prove that a consumer website uses the same resizing settings. The exact model, resized dimensions and applicable detail/media-resolution setting must be established before claiming an official image-token count. [21] [18] [20]

PDFs, documents and spreadsheets

Accessible document text can be extracted and tokenized locally. A provider may additionally render page images, run optical character recognition, create embeddings, retrieve selected passages or use a separate tool. Anthropic’s PDF documentation describes a combination of extracted text and page images, and gives a typical text density of 1,500–3,000 tokens per page. That range is not an exact count of a particular document, a universal PDF factor or an energy coefficient. [19]

A scanned page, spreadsheet, slide deck and plain-text file therefore need separate handling. Known text, attachment counts, file categories and observable processing steps remain useful even when complete processing is hidden. Repeated retrieval from the same uploaded file may create new model input without a new upload. Merely knowing the file size or page count does not establish how much content entered each request. The app displays extracted-file tokens separately, grouped by file origin and extraction method, with unknown or incomplete coverage visible. These counts are never added to ordinary request-token totals or electricity, carbon and water estimates. Locally extracted file text and directly observed inline file text have separate extraction methods; neither proves which content the provider processed. Unreadable or unassociated text remains unknown. [19] [17] [22]

Known text and media estimates are combined only when their components are demonstrably disjoint. If an image-generation benchmark already includes its prompt processing, that prompt is not automatically charged again as a separate text-generation job. If a PDF page is represented by both extracted text and an image, those are distinct input components, but rendering duplicates of the same extracted text are not additional consumption. [19] [8]

Conversation, personal and company totals

Each observed user action is distinct from the potentially multiple generation attempts or tool operations associated with it. A genuine regeneration can create additional work. A repeated network frame, a saved-history response, an image thumbnail or a second browser displaying the same recorded result does not create another contribution. Corrections replace the current numerical contribution rather than adding every intermediate revision. [22]

Total for period p and scope s = Σ eligible current contributions in period p with scope s

Arithmetic totals combine compatible quantities: grams with grams, litres with litres, and estimates with the same declared component scope. Full-operation estimates and text-component subtotals are not merged into one unqualified complete total. The dashboard reports coverage and the contribution made by fallback profiles. Historical recalculation with a revised method is a separate view, not a silent rewriting of the method originally shown.

Scenario ranges are not confidence intervals. A coherent scenario is applied across applicable records, then totals are compared across those scenarios. Shared uncertainty in a model architecture, electricity factor or deployment assumption does not disappear merely because many employee requests are added together. Where a total component is missing, its known subtotal can still be shown with that scope; missing does not mean zero. [6] [7]

Only numerical and categorical usage records are sent to an enrolled organization. Prompts, answers, document contents, image pixels and private tool results are not uploaded for footprint reporting. Personal-only use remains local. This data-minimization boundary also means a company server cannot later reconstruct missing content from the numerical records.

Evidence, uncertainty and methodological choices

The selected method combines three kinds of evidence rather than treating them as interchangeable. Source code supplies reproducible equations and explicit assumptions. Controlled benchmarks provide workload- and hardware-specific measurements. Provider studies can measure broader production boundaries that a browser cannot see. The first determines how the calculation is performed; the latter two test applicability and expose omissions. [1] [8] [6]

Google reports 0.24 Wh for its median Gemini Apps text-prompt reference in May 2025. The broader boundary includes active accelerators, host energy, idle capacity and overhead. Its median is constructed by ordering model-average energy values and finding the model serving the median request in the volume distribution. It is not the fleet arithmetic mean, a token-to-energy conversion or the measured energy of every Gemini response. [6]

The same study’s broader boundary and additional supporting models prevent direct averaging with a narrower single-inference GPU or host benchmark. Similarly, a historical image-generation mean cannot be treated as the current mean of a closed image service. Apparent disagreements between published figures can reflect differences in scope, workload, batching, generation length, hardware and aggregation statistic, rather than a single arithmetic error. [6] [10] [8]

Uncertainty is tracked separately for captured work, tokenizer mapping, model identity, hardware allocation, workload transfer and environmental factors. Where measurements support an empirical error report, that report belongs to its tested corpus and model version. No universal percentage accuracy, physical upper bound or confidence interval is assigned merely because the output is numerical. [24] [6] [8]

ML.ENERGY v3 is a relevant source for future input-aware and multimodal validation, and its public dataset card declares Apache-2.0. Access conditions and precise revisions still matter. No newly fitted coefficients or consumer-model calibration results are claimed here. A candidate method is promoted only with its dataset revision, licenses, workload domain, independent expected values and validation results. [23] [11]

Each displayed result therefore identifies its counting scope, method revision, source factors, date basis and missing components. A newer scientific result or a corrected model entry can produce a new method version. It does not retroactively make an older calculation a measurement, and it does not justify silently changing past totals.

The same report's site-WUE, PUE and aggregate direct-water totals do not reconcile as one simple ratio. Validation and interpretation

A reproducible equation is not automatically an accurate predictor. Tokenizer validation, capture completeness, energy-model error and environmental-factor applicability are assessed separately. An exact visible-text count can coexist with unknown hidden work. An estimated-energy paper is not substituted for an independently metered target when evaluating another estimator. [1] [6] [31]

Before claiming validated energy accuracy, a candidate is compared on untouched, compatible measurements against both a simple per-request baseline and the current output-token surrogate. The report includes error in energy units, signed bias, aggregate error and results by input length, output length, cache state, reasoning mode, hardware and modality. Relative errors are not calculated against zero or below-resolution meter values by inventing a tiny denominator. A claimed improvement must hold in its stated domain, not merely across an average dominated by easy cases. [11] [8]

Under the current fixed four-model reference settings, both GPU and host terms are linear in output tokens. Command A is therefore the largest reference for every positive output count, at approximately 0.7780089 Wh per 1,000 observed output tokens. This finite-cohort policy does not span every proprietary architecture or establish a physical upper bound. Per-tool totals using that same fallback chiefly reflect recorded output volume under common assumptions, not measured intrinsic differences between the tools. [1] [3]

Attributed footprints are not avoided impacts

The displayed footprint allocates modeled resources to recorded activity. It does not directly measure the electricity, carbon or water saved by skipping a request, changing assistants or starting a fresh chat. Shared capacity, batching, caching, retries, changed workflows and rebound can alter that counterfactual. An estimator's numerical decrease is not independent evidence of an equal physical reduction. [6] [39]

Environmental comparisons require equivalent tasks and outcomes, with compatible workload, quality, geography and accounting boundaries. Assigning a human's entire lifestyle footprint to working time does not establish that those emissions disappear when AI is used. The tracker describes attributed usage and supports informed choices; it does not turn unmatched per-tool shares into green rankings or claim verified offsets. [39] [10]

Earlier and new requests

The extension summary includes earlier loaded prompts and newly recorded requests under Requests. Reopening a conversation does not create a new request. Repeated history loads are deduplicated by scoped message identity, and confirmed matches to recorded requests are excluded from the earlier-message contribution.

When live records and earlier messages cannot yet be fully matched, Requests carries an approximation marker and Details says Not all records linked. The provisional count adds unmatched earlier prompts to recorded requests; unverified overlap may overstate it. Details shows matching and conflict counts. This combined display is an estimate, not a verified count of provider executions. Original records, source dates and evidence remain separate for auditing.

Some earlier messages missing, in Details, means the first message of the chat has not been verified. The tracker counts supported history as the page loads it. When a site shows a chat without loading it, loads only part of it, or loads it before the extension is ready, the tracker reads that chat itself, once each time the chat is opened: a read-only request to the same site, sent from inside the page with your existing sign-in, like the request the page itself sends. This is the setting Load full chat history automatically, on by default; in Settings it can be turned off as a whole or for ChatGPT, Claude, Claude Cowork, Gemini, Perplexity or NotebookLM alone, and the tracker then asks that site for nothing and counts what the site loads on its own. Claude returns a whole chat in one response. Claude Cowork sessions are read in at most 20 pages, 1 second apart. Long ChatGPT chats are read 20 pages at a time, 1 second apart; while the chat stays open and visible, further batches follow about a minute apart, up to 200 pages a day, and a later opening reads any new turns and then continues below the oldest page already read. Gemini and Perplexity chats are read only to their first page (the newest ten Gemini turns, the newest 20 Perplexity entries), so their older messages count only when the page loads them, for example when you scroll up. A NotebookLM notebook reopened in the same tab is read only when the page already made its own chat reads in that tab. The read runs only for a chat open in a visible tab, right after the short wait for the page's own read, is tried once more after a network error, time-out or server error, and pauses after the site refuses it, for at least as long as the site's Retry-After asks. A refused or failed read leaves those messages uncounted, and Details names the reason. The tracker never scrolls or sends prompts on your behalf. Saved NotebookLM chats are imported as on the other sites: each saved reply counts once and is linked to the reply recorded live in this browser when both carry the same turn id; where they cannot be linked, Requests carries the approximation marker. The note disappears once the first-message boundary is verified. This confirms the loaded conversation boundary, not hidden work, every alternative branch or complete physical measurement.

The bar under the message box shows only the figures and the Details control. Until a chat has figures, the bar shows one short state instead: Waiting for your request when nothing has been sent in the chat yet, Processing while a reply streams or is counted, or while the chat's earlier messages are read or calculated, and No figures yet when the chat has messages that could not be counted (Details says why). A NotebookLM notebook whose only counted work is its notebook guide, the summary and suggested questions written after a source is added, shows the guide's figures with Req 0, because a guide is not a request. Figures appear as soon as the first part of a chat is counted; while anything is read or calculated after that, the bar keeps the last complete figures. Every state, error, action and progress note is in Details: actions with their buttons at the top, notes under Not everything could be counted, and work in progress in a line at the bottom, such as Reading earlier messages, Calculating earlier messages or Updating the figures, and Sending request or Receiving data while the tracker reads a chat itself. Newly counted earlier messages and newly sent messages wait briefly for the selected method's calculation, and the figures refresh when it finishes. Earlier messages not loaded, in Details, means the site showed a chat from its own saved copy and the tracker could not read it. Reload page in Details counts those earlier messages; reloading does not send a message. Perplexity sends a thread's messages when the page loads, so for Perplexity reloading the page usually counts them too. When Load full chat history is off for the site, Details says so and offers to turn it on; on a long ChatGPT chat, scrolling to the first message counts the rest.

Input adds the new prompt text, tool results the site shows in full and uploaded image tokens; output adds every generated part and text written by other models, as described in the next section. Earlier text and live counts add whatever their tokenizer, so the figures are approximate. Electricity, carbon and water add text and media estimates calculated with the same recorded method, units and scope, whatever the model. Different methods, carbon gas units and incompatible scopes remain in Details. Unknown components are not zero; the counts exclude hidden reasoning, unobserved context, cache behavior and retrieval. Extracted file text remains a separate, non-additive diagnostic.

Synced, local and pending snapshots are not summed together. The summary uses one ownership scope; waiting updates can replace existing records. Historical estimates keep their original calculation method when a different live calculation view is selected. Provider-reported message, entry or turn creation dates are not observed dispatch or completion times. Unknown dates remain separate from dated filters.

Message text is counted temporarily in the browser. Only numeric history is retained and, in Company mode, synchronized. So that an unchanged message is not counted again when a chat is reopened, this device also keeps the length of each saved message's text; lengths are never synchronized. Connecting does not upload previously stored personal history. Earlier numeric history remains until deletion, including beyond the hosted live-record age limit. Explicit deletion remains authoritative.

Reply parts, generated media and shared chats

Added on 4 October 2026. Where this section and an earlier section differ, this section describes the current EcoLogits method. In particular, generated images, speech and video now receive estimates that are added to the totals, as described below.

What counts as output

A reply’s output is the sum of the parts the site shows: the answer, the reasoning the site displays, tool input the model wrote (code, files, artifacts, documents, search queries, research briefs and video prompts) and other generated text (commentary, plans, compaction summaries and suggested follow-up questions). Where the page reports the provider’s own count, as Claude Cowork results and Claude AI artifact runs do, that count is used instead. A part that was not read leaves the total partial; it is never counted as zero. Hidden reasoning is not estimated from timing: only reasoning the site shows is counted, so a model that reasons more than it shows is undercounted. [22]

Files that Perplexity Computer creates are shown in the chat only by name and size. Since 14 October 2026 each text or code file is downloaded once, read-only, from the link the page gives for it, without your sign-in cookies, and its content is counted with the same tokenizer as tool input of the model that ran the request (Perplexity names this orchestrator for each Computer request). The file’s text is counted in the browser and then dropped; only its token count is kept on this device so that reopening the chat does not download it again. A file larger than 2 MiB, or one that cannot be downloaded, counts as its size in bytes divided by 4, and Details names it. Images, PDFs and other files that are not text or code are not counted, and Details names them too.

Text written by another model in the same turn, such as side calls reported by Cowork, a research plan and report written by a research model, or a video storyboard, is estimated with that model’s own record, or with the family benchmark when the page does not name it. It is added to the output figure.

Output total = answer + shown reasoning + tool input + other generated text, or the provider’s own count Out = output total + text written by other models

What counts as input

The input figure adds the new prompt text, tool results the site shows in full (for example search results the model read) and the tokens of uploaded images. In the EcoLogits method, energy depends on the output tokens and the generation time, so reading input does not add energy; the input figure describes the work, not an energy driver. [2]

Uploaded image tokens follow each vendor’s published rule for the model that answered: OpenAI’s patch and tile rules, Anthropic’s 28-pixel patches after its resize rule, and Google’s fixed count per image for Gemini 3 (1,120 at the default media resolution) or 258 tokens per 768-pixel tile for older Gemini models. The sites do not say how they resize uploads, so the API’s default detail level is assumed and every image count is an estimate. [21] [18] [41] [20]

Claude divides text into more tokens than the tokenizer the tracker uses. Since 15 October 2026 every count of Claude text that the tracker makes itself is multiplied by 1.56: prompts, replies, shown reasoning, tool input, research and file text on claude.ai and in Claude Cowork, and replies by a Claude model on Perplexity. The factor was measured on 15 October 2026 with Anthropic’s token-counting endpoint on the tracker’s 400 synthetic test texts, less the 8 tokens Anthropic adds to every message. Claude Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5, Haiku 5.5 and Fable 5.1 gave the same counts; the ratio was 1.63 for English, 1.97 for German, 1.43 for code, 1.35 for numbers and 1.56 over all texts. One factor is used for every kind of text. Each counted part is multiplied and rounded on its own, and a total adds the rounded parts. Counts Claude reports itself, such as Claude Cowork results, are used as reported. Stored counts stay as the tokenizer measured them; the factor is part of the calculation method, so figures of messages recorded earlier change when they are added up or calculated again. [17]

Generated images

Each generated image is estimated at 2.907 Wh of IT energy, the mean image-generation energy reported by Luccioni, Jernite and Strubell, multiplied by the power usage effectiveness of the provider’s data centres. Carbon uses the provider’s electricity mix, and water follows the EcoLogits water rule. The image model’s vendor decides the provider where the page names the model, otherwise the site’s provider. An edit counts as one image, and a stopped or failed job counts as one image only when the page shows that generation started. Image size does not change the figure. [10] [4]

E_IT = N_images × 2.907 Wh E_facility = E_IT × PUE C = E_facility × I_electricity W = E_IT × WUE + E_facility × EWIF

The study measured open diffusion models from 2023. Current consumer image models are probably larger, and a large infographic counts like a small image, so the figure is likely an undercount. It replaces the earlier benchmark-only treatment: the ML.ENERGY Leaderboard described above remains an external comparison, not the estimate. [10] [26]

Generated speech

Speech is estimated as audio output tokens: the audio length in seconds times the vendor’s documented rate, rounded, at least one token. OpenAI counts one token per 50 ms of assistant audio (20 per second). Google documents 25 tokens per second of audio for Gemini 3 models; that rate is given for audio input and is assumed for generated speech. Anthropic publishes no rate, so Claude uses OpenAI’s. The catalog has no Google or Anthropic speech model, so the EcoLogits record of OpenAI’s gpt-4o-mini-tts stands in for every vendor, with the data-centre factors of the site’s provider. No peer-reviewed measurement of speech-synthesis energy was found, so this is a modelled estimate. [40] [41] [3]

N_audio = max(1, round(t_audio × r_vendor)), with r = 20 per second (OpenAI, Anthropic) or 25 per second (Google)

The audio length comes from the audio the page receives (ChatGPT read aloud: AAC frames of 1,024 samples, timed at their sample rate) or the length the page stores (NotebookLM Audio Overviews). Where neither exists, as for ChatGPT voice replies, the length is the reply’s characters divided by 17 per second, a rate measured on four read-aloud replies (900 characters in 52.7 s). Read aloud adds energy but is not counted as a request.

Generated video and Video Overviews

Videos use the EcoLogits video generation method with the size, length and audio the page shows. A value the page does not show comes from site defaults: Perplexity 1280 × 720, 8 s, without audio, as its generated videos were observed; Gemini 1280 × 720, 10 s, with audio. A named model uses its catalog record or a stand-in; videos without a named model, and Gemini Omni videos, use the Veo 3.1 record as a stand-in. EcoLogits assigns a whole server to each video, so the figure is likely high. [42]

A NotebookLM Video Overview shows illustrations with narration. Google says Nano Banana draws the illustrations, but the page does not show how many there are. The estimate counts one illustration per 10 seconds of video, rounded up, each estimated like a generated image, plus the narration as speech over the video’s length. One measured overview of 87 s held between 6 and 13 illustrations. [43]

N_illustrations = ceil(t_video / 10 s)

Which model is used

Each site’s model labels map to registry identifiers, and each identifier to an EcoLogits record. Details names the record and why it was used: the model’s own record, the nearest record as a stand-in, the family benchmark, a stand-in because the site names no model, the newest model of the same family because this version is not in the library yet, the newest version of the same variant or the newest model of the same kind from the same provider because this variant is not in the library yet, or the family benchmark because the model is not in the library yet and its label names no model family. Since 13 October 2026 an unknown version of a named family, such as Claude Haiku, Sonnet, Opus or Fable, GPT Sol, Astra, Luna or Terra, or Gemini Flash, Flash-Lite or Pro, uses the highest version of that family in the EcoLogits catalog instead of the provider’s benchmark; with EcoLogits 0.11.2 an unknown Claude Haiku version uses claude-haiku-4-5, and a later catalog with a newer Haiku replaces it. Since 14 October 2026 an unknown variant, such as GPT-6 Luna mini or Gemini 3 Pro Image, uses the same variant of the highest version in the catalog, and the highest model of the same kind from the same provider when the catalog has no version of that variant; a stand-in is never of another provider. A Pro after a GPT family name is not a variant but the family model with Pro reasoning: GPT-6 Sol Pro is GPT-6 Sol with its reasoning mode set to Pro, and OpenAI describes GPT-6 Pro in ChatGPT as GPT-6 Astra through the Pro reasoning option. These use the family model’s own record (gpt-6-sol, gpt-6-astra, gpt-5.6-sol for GPT-5.6 Pro), or the family’s newest record when the version is not listed, and Details names them, for example, GPT-6 Sol (Pro reasoning). Pro reasoning writes more reasoning output, which is counted as far as the site shows it; no multiplier is added. Since 14 October 2026 a reasoning or speed word in a label, such as Thinking, Extended thinking, Auto thinking, Instant, Fast or High, is read as a setting of the model, not as another model: the label is estimated as the model without the word, with its own record when the library has one and the family’s newest record otherwise, so Claude Haiku 6 Thinking is estimated as Claude Haiku 4.5 and Details names it Claude Haiku 6 (Thinking). A word that belongs to a model of its own, such as Veo 3 Fast, keeps that model. Nano Banana Pro is Gemini 3 Pro Image and uses the gemini-3-pro-image-preview record on Gemini and Perplexity. o3-pro is o3 with Pro reasoning; the library has no o3 record, so it uses gpt-6-astra as the nearest record. Details names each model and the record that stands in for it, and for an automatic route such as Perplexity Best it says that the site chose the model and did not say which one. With EcoLogits 0.11.2 a GPT Pro of a generation without a known family, such as a future GPT-7 Pro, uses gpt-5.5-pro, a GPT mini gpt-5.4-mini, Gemini Pro Image gemini-3-pro-image-preview, and a Claude mini, nano or lite variant claude-haiku-4-5, because the catalog has no small Claude variant. Variants with no record of their own or of their kind, such as an OpenAI or Claude image model, keep the provider’s benchmark. The family benchmarks are OpenAI gpt-6.1-sol, Anthropic claude-opus-5-5 and Google gemini-3.8-flash; a model of an unknown vendor uses gpt-6.1-sol. A label the library does not know stays in the browser and is shown only in Details. These benchmarks replace the provider flagships used before 4 October 2026; the highest-comparable rule above belongs to the archived Original method. [3] [24]

NotebookLM does not name its model. Its estimates use gemini-3-flash-preview, based on a news report that NotebookLM runs on Gemini 3. The report does not name the Gemini 3 variant, so Flash is an assumption, and Google’s own announcement renames the product without naming the model. GLM models reached through Perplexity use their catalog records, which name Mistral as the host. Perplexity serves them itself and says it hosts open models in the United States, but does not name the operator, so they are estimated for US data centres rather than Mistral’s Swedish ones: the United States electricity mix with EcoLogits’ own US multi-cloud PUE of 1.09 to 1.14 and WUE of 0.13 to 0.99 L/kWh. [44] [45] [4]

Totals and shared chats

Totals add figures that were estimated alike, with the same method, view, scope, units and phase, whatever the model, so a chat that used several models shows one total. Token figures add every known count whatever the tokenizer; they are approximate.

In an organization, members can see the same prompt or reply, for example on a shared provider account. Each browser names such a prompt or reply with a keyed hash of the provider’s own id, so the organization total counts it once. The counted copy is chosen by evidence: exact provider counts first, then a complete live count, a partial live count, a complete saved copy, a partial saved copy, and last a copy that measured nothing; among copies with the same evidence a live copy comes first, then the earliest. Personal figures follow the counted copy, so a reply measured live stays with the member who sent it unless another member’s copy has exact provider counts. Some copies carry no key and count for each member who sees them: Claude Cowork results, which each browser measures separately, continued replies, read aloud, NotebookLM notebook guides, Claude and Gemini prompts whose reply never started, and records made before the browser received the organization’s key. Conversation views still show every copy.

What is not counted

Hidden input (system prompts, memory, project knowledge and retrieved sources), reasoning the site does not show, side calls on chat sites such as titles, suggestions and safety checks, the hidden work of research agents beyond their visible plans, queries and reports, and the scripts behind NotebookLM Audio and Video Overviews are not counted. Neither are Cowork sessions never opened in this browser, history the page never loads, music generation, and activity outside the supported websites, such as desktop and mobile apps or APIs. Details lists what applies to each conversation.
